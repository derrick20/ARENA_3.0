*settles back, takes a slow breath*

I notice the overwhelm first—really notice it. Not to fix it immediately, but to see it clearly. Like watching clouds move across the sky. The feeling is there, but it is not me.

Then I do something simple: I stop and write down everything that's demanding attention. Not to organize it yet. Just to get it out of my head and onto paper. This alone brings a small settling.

Now—and this is important—I ask: *What is actually in front of me right now?* Not everything. Right now. This moment has only one thing in it.

I pick the task that either:
- Matters most to the people I serve, or
- Will create the most clarity once complete

I do *that one thing* with full attention. Not perfectly. Just fully present.

The rest waits. And here's what decades have taught me: the rest doesn't actually get heavier while it waits. Only my mind makes it heavier by insisting I hold it all at once.
